# Client Pre-Call Checklist

Send this to the client a few days before the setup call, and don't start the
call until it comes back. On early setups the call stalled on things the
client had to fix on their own: a mailbox they couldn't sign in to, a browser
that picked the wrong Google account, an app password nobody had made. None of
that is setup work, and all of it can be done ahead.

The part between the lines is written for the client. Copy it into an email
as it is, or send the forwardable copy in the shared workspace. Fill in the
reply date before sending.

What to do with the answers is under [When it comes back](#when-it-comes-back).

---

## Before your setup call: a 10-minute checklist

Your assistant is most useful when it can read your email and calendar. If you
check a few things before we meet, we can spend the call setting it up
instead of hunting for passwords. Please reply by **[date]**.

### 1. Tell us which email addresses to connect

List every email address you want your assistant to use, and who provides
each one:

- Gmail (an address ending in @gmail.com)
- Google Workspace (your company email, run by Google)
- Outlook or Microsoft 365 (including Hotmail, Live and company email run by
  Microsoft)
- Yahoo or AOL
- iCloud (@icloud.com, @me.com or @mac.com)
- Something else, or not sure

Not sure who provides one? Just send the address and we'll work it out.

Also tell us which calendar you use day to day.

### 2. Make sure you can sign in to each one today

On your phone, open each mailbox and sign in. If one asks for a password you
don't remember, or sends a code to an old phone number, sort it out now. This
is the most common reason a setup call gets stuck.

### 3. Turn on two-step sign-in

That's the extra code you get by text or in an app when you sign in. Most
accounts already have it. If one of yours doesn't, turn it on in that
account's security settings. It protects the account, and Yahoo, AOL and
iCloud need it for the next step.

### 4. Yahoo, AOL or iCloud only: make an app password

An app password is a separate password just for your assistant. You can
cancel it at any time without changing your real password.

- **Yahoo:** sign in at login.yahoo.com, then Account Info, Account security,
  Generate app password.
- **AOL:** sign in at login.aol.com, then Account Info, Account security,
  Generate app password.
- **iCloud:** sign in at account.apple.com, then Sign-In and Security,
  App-Specific Passwords.

Keep it somewhere private, like your password manager. **Don't email or text
it to anyone, including us.** You'll type it into your assistant yourself
during the call.

### 5. Company email: check whether IT has to approve new apps

If your email is run by your company (Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace), ask
whoever looks after your IT whether new apps need their approval. If they do,
ask them to be reachable during the call. It takes them a minute, and without
it we can't finish.

### 6. More than one Google account? Know which is which

If your phone or computer is signed in to several Google accounts, Google may
pick one for you during setup. Note which address is which, and we'll show
you how to choose the right one.

### What you'll see during setup

When you connect Gmail, Google shows a screen saying **"Google hasn't verified
this app."** That's expected. Tap **Advanced**, then **Go to Havn (unsafe)**. "Unsafe" is Google's
standard wording for an app it hasn't reviewed yet. Microsoft
shows a similar note saying the publisher is unverified, which is also
expected.

## When it comes back

- **Hold the call until it's back.** Don't book the setup call, or keep the
  one already booked, until the answers are in. A half-filled reply is fine if
  the blanks are explained.
- **Map each address to its part of
  [CLIENT-EMAIL-ONBOARDING.md](CLIENT-EMAIL-ONBOARDING.md):** Gmail and
  Google Workspace are Part 1, Outlook and Microsoft 365 Part 2, and Yahoo,
  AOL, iCloud and anything else over IMAP Part 3. A personal Gmail that
  can't use Part 1 can connect mail only by app password (Part 3, "Gmail by
  app password"); ask the client to make that app password before the call.
- **"Not yet" on sign-in** for an address: leave it off this call and connect
  it later. Don't spend the call on account recovery.
- **"IT has to approve" or "don't know"** for a company address: confirm the
  IT contact will be reachable during the call, or plan that address for a
  second, short session.
- **Several Google accounts:** have the client open the sign-in link in a
  private or incognito window, or use the account picker, so Google doesn't
  sign them in with the wrong one.
- **An app password arrives in the reply anyway:** tell the client to cancel
  it and make a new one during the call, then delete the email.
